# drumroll state of the world — 2026-08-12

audit cutoff: `fa5b9ca3bb8681141a010fdedf328956e846ec21` (`My Wave`).

this is an endpoint audit, not a credit ledger. a source change, an old screenshot, or a passing unit test earns no claim that the owner can sit at a Yamaha DTX402 and learn well today. current owner feedback and the persisted on-device practice data outrank earlier release notes where they disagree.

## bottom line

drumroll has a substantial implementation: a native Electron app, a lesson corpus, a tutor state machine, score modes, song rows, persisted practice evidence, and a growing release history. that is real work. the core product promise is still unproven and, on three fresh signals, currently broken:

- a guided `lesson:01.01` run on 2026-08-12 reached `0.5x` even though its stored run has 131 hits, one miss, and 99.24% hit accuracy on `DTX Drums`.
- the immediately following fresh song checkpoint recorded zero hits and zero wrong hits, while recording seven misses. that is direct evidence that the song judge did not persist the played-strike stream correctly; it does not prove that the DTX402 stopped emitting MIDI.
- the owner reports that the Flow playhead renders ahead of the audible transport. the present test suite proves geometry cases, not live audio-clock agreement.

therefore the honest claim at this cutoff is: _the app contains tutor and practice machinery; it has not yet earned the claim that a connected DTX402 player can reliably start, be judged, recover, and improve without laptop intervention._

## drift, reversals, and claims that need to be retired

### later owner feedback overrides earlier design assumptions

1. the early request was “one hit starts the experience.” the later correction is more precise: one hit should start deliberately, not trigger surprise restarts or force a premature song transition. treat the latter as the current contract.
2. an earlier pedagogical instinct pushed forward progression; the owner later said that a struggling beginner should keep working the lesson rather than be ejected into songs. the next-item policy must preserve lesson context until a humanly sensible completion point.
3. `design-direction-v2.md` favors a thin local pause band over obscuring the score. the later owner request asks for a clearly full-screen pause state that yields when mouse control resumes. the newer request wins.
4. Flow and Classic started as two alternative renderings. the owner now asks for an explicit setting-level decision, with Flow zoom and phrase visibility. coexistence alone is insufficient.

### implementation/document drift

1. the requirement ledger’s kb.7 matrix says the tutor floor is `0.7x`; the active guided settings and current source permit `0.5x`, and the current persisted lesson reached it. the ledger claim is false at this cutoff.
2. the ledger reports tolerant recovery and stable Flow as passed. the fresh live data and owner report contradict both claims. retain the old checks as historical evidence only.
3. `pedagogy-engine-v2.md` correctly says My Wave does not alter live song/lesson/score screens. calling `fa5b9ca` a shipped personalized practice experience would therefore be misleading.
4. “all playlist songs” drifted into a tiny playable subset: three rows work, eight await exact lawful audio, two have incomplete identity, and the broader 230-song request remains unmet.
5. the visual direction says “one dominant object” and “no card salad.” the owner’s current visual feedback says the rendered product still reads as modular. source intention is ahead of visual outcome.
6. kb.10 materials describe integrated mechanics, while the current code version remains kb.11 and the latest commit only adds My Wave service code. do not imply that the current installed app contains a verified newer release.

## what actually matters next

ranked for one person with a DTX402 and about one month to learn drums:

1. make every real pad strike arrive, map, judge, persist, and visibly react in both lesson and song mode. prove a fresh ten-hit DTX402 song run has ten expected judge events, a readable score reaction, and a saved result. no recommendation, polish, or expansion matters until this is true.
2. replace the punitive speed policy with a musical intervention policy. no automatic descent below the agreed floor without an explicit user choice; distinguish timing spread from inability, preserve the phrase, and produce a targeted short loop rather than repeated speed collapse.
3. put Flow on one authoritative transport clock. prove the drawn marker, audio position, pause/resume, tempo change, and loop boundaries against the same timestamp at 0.7x, 1x, and any adaptive speed. a fixed-playhead screenshot is insufficient.
4. make the first ten minutes hands-free and coherent. connected status, one deliberate strike/pedal start, clear count-in, no surprise restart, no forced song jump, and a direct way to repeat a bar.
5. reduce the home and score surfaces to a single at-kit decision. open/resume the right exercise from the kit, make Flow/Classic explicit, and fit the readable kit/score at the owner’s actual screen distance. visual work earns priority only where it removes a wrong action.
6. finish the learning mechanics that compound practice. drag-select a phrase, pause correctly, expose notation help, then show full-screen post-run feedback tied to one next action. these are high-value only after p0 correctness.
7. connect My Wave to a visible, accountable recommendation. give one recommendation whose explanation names the favorite-song section, observed weakness, chosen tempo, and completion criterion; then verify it changes after a run.
8. complete content breadth only where it is lawful and playable. keep a precise playlist rights/identity ledger, add the next playable songs, and stop presenting catalog manifests as a usable library.
9. defer Arena/Resonance/P2 retention embellishment until the saved data is trustworthy. a large streak or reminder on corrupt judgment data trains the wrong habit and damages trust.
