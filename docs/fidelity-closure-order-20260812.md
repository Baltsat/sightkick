## what he actually wants

He does not want a drum-learning dashboard with a good recommendation subsystem. He wants a credible practice instrument beside his kit.

The product has to make four facts immediately legible:

1. the kit is connected and the next strike will do the expected thing;
2. the score tells him what to play, the colours tell him where to hit, and a mistake tells him how to repair it;
3. the tutor has his back: it will not punish a beginner, steal tempo, or kick him out of the work he chose;
4. after a run, he can see one honest musical change and one credible next action.

The unspoken requirement is a **trust budget**. Every false XP number, unexplained restart, drifting playhead, and dead library row spends it. Once that budget is gone, more adaptive logic reads as manipulation instead of help. The useful bar is simple: he should be able to explain, from the screen and his own hands, why the app did what it did.

He also wants agency without administration. He wants the system to make strong defaults from evidence, then let him override one thing — repeat this bar, keep this speed, stay in this lesson — without being pushed through a product flow. That is why the first ten minutes and the post-run receipt matter more than another route, card, or ranking model.

## what to remove or hide until it earns a place

- The home XP goal/flame strip and every metric that cannot reconcile to a saved run.
- The separate Coach identity, plus its cards; move evidence into the exact decision moment.
- “Daybreak Arena” and “Learn the motion. Own the room.” They are brand/motivation noise at the kit.
- The home split-grid/card composition, technical source modes, and first-view import controls.
- The large practice inspector as the default reading surface; settings should be reachable, not occupy the musical field.
- Automatic speed reductions, restarts, and route changes that occur without a clear learner choice.
- Any library “play” implication before audio, chart, and launch proof are green.
- More gamification mechanics until truthful run evidence and the musical receipt are working.

## shared refactor notes for integration

- Do not repair visual home fidelity inside individual feature components. `HomeCockpit`, `KitHome.css`, `AppShell`, and the header/status composition need one owner; otherwise cards and counters will reappear through props.
- Treat `SongView` as the single join point for transport, judge evidence, tutor policy, and the post-run receipt. A cosmetic score patch cannot fix a false persisted run.
- Keep library source truth separate from the simple song shelf. The user needs one collection; engineers still need provenance and proof gates behind each row.
- The visual-system document says pause should be a local edge state, while the later owner ask explicitly requests a dark full-screen state that yields on pointer input. The later owner ask wins. Keep score context visible enough to resume; do not reintroduce an opaque failure modal.
